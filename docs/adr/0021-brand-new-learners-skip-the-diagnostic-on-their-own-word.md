# Brand-new Learners skip the diagnostic on their own word

Before the Starting Level diagnostic, the Course Guide asks how much the Learner knows already. **Brand new to this** skips the eight items. The Request gets a diagnostic attempt with no items and a Starting Level that is a floor with no probed capabilities, marked `selfReportedNew`, and moves straight to `starting_level_confirmed`. **I know some of it** prepares the diagnostic as before. This amends spec §4.3, which ruled out a Starting Level from self-report.

A real newcomer would answer every item with I don't know. That gives the Blueprint the same floor, after a minute or two of preparation and eight questions that tell the Learner they know nothing. The claim being skipped is a claim of absence, which is the one self-report a Learner can make reliably. Self-report is still never used to treat a capability as **evidenced**; only a correct Diagnostic item does that.

What §4.3 guards against still holds. The Starting Level is not dressed up as a diagnostic result: it says it is the Learner's word, and no capability is marked evidenced or not evidenced. The Blueprint is told the Learner is new and starts from the fundamentals of the whole Learning Goal, within its Goal scope.

Rejected:
- **Prepare the diagnostic while asking, and discard it on Brand new.** Faster for I know some of it, but it pays for a full item set, Judge, and fact-check for every newcomer.
- **Three levels (new, some, a lot) that steer item difficulty.** The items already span novice to near-goal; a self-rated level would bias the decomposition without evidence.
- **Store it as eight skipped items.** That would look like a completed diagnostic, which is what §4.3 forbids.
