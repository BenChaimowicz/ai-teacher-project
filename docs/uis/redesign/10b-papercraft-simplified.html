Lesson 7

Yggdrasil and the nine worlds

Norse myth doesn't picture the universe as a sphere or a flat disc. It pictures a tree. Yggdrasil is an evergreen ash whose branches spread over the sky, and every world, from the gods' hall to the frozen dark, sits somewhere along its trunk or among its roots.

Three roots, three wells

In Snorri Sturluson's account, the tree stands on three roots. One reaches the gods in Asgard, where the Well of Urd waters it each day. One reaches Mímir's well of wisdom, the one Odin gave an eye to drink from. The third sinks into misty Niflheim, where the dragon Níðhöggr gnaws at it from below.1

1 · Snorri Sturluson, Prose Edda, Gylfaginning 15 (c. 1220)

Worth knowing

No medieval source lists all nine worlds in one place. The tidy list you see in books and games is a modern reconstruction.

A squirrel called Ratatoskr runs up and down the trunk, carrying insults between the eagle at the top and the dragon at the bottom.

04

The direction, briefly

  • Why it works. Paper is the material of studying, so the product feels warm and trustworthy without looking cute. On a dark base, a few flat sheets read as calm and premium.
  • Restraint as the rule. There's one shadow, one accent, two UI typefaces plus a reading face, and one motif. If something carries no information, it doesn't ship.
  • The hamster. It has seven layers: a backdrop disc, ears, body, cream front, feet, face and a ribbon tail. Its silhouette holds up at 16px, and the cap only appears for wins.
  • Motion. Layers unfold softly, breathing runs on a 5s cycle, and blinks are slow. A win is a 1.6s lift and settle with a handful of paper flecks. Parallax stays within 4°.
  • Built in Rive. One artboard with seven Z-ordered groups. The states are Idle, Thinking, Win (a one-shot that returns to Idle) and Resting. lookX and lookY inputs drive the tilt and the pupils.
