# The Starting Level author writes for a first-round pass, and the Judge's distractor bar is a non-knower's, not an expert's

**Why:** Only 57% of Sol's items passed the first review (56 items, seven checks since 2026-11-01), so almost every check paid for a rewrite round, and the slowest checks were the ones that paid for several. Seventeen of twenty Judge flaws were the same one, in Luna's words "implausible distractors, making the answer easy to guess", and all seven code failures were the key repeating a stem word. The author's rules said "plausible distractors" without saying what that meant, while the Judge was applying its own bar; Sol wrote to its instructions. Four changes, all on the author's side of the Judge except the last: the rules now define a distractor the way the Judge checks it (a belief a Learner with partial knowledge would hold; not something a non-knower could rule out) with one example; the author must name the misconception behind each distractor in the output, because a model that has to write the belief cannot write "millimeters" for a bacterial cell; the two machine checks are stated literally so they stop being surprises; and an item whose stem and key the Judge found sound keeps them through the rewrite and gets two new wrong options, instead of a whole new item that can fail in new ways. The Judge's own definition of the distractor flaw is tightened to match: a distractor is flawed only when a Learner who knows nothing of the subject could rule it out as absurd, unsafe, off-topic, or contradicting the stem; one that is merely weaker than the key is not. Ben accepted that the wrong options may be a little less demanding in exchange for checks that finish in one round. The alternatives were exemplar items in the prompt (held back: anchoring risk, and tokens on every call) and more author reasoning tokens (SEN-125 already found the cap worthwhile, and distractor craft is not a depth problem). What it costs: a few dozen output tokens per item for the misconceptions; a Judge that lets a weak-but-not-absurd distractor through; a stored review gains an optional `newDistractorsWouldFix` field.
