# Readings ask the Learner no questions anywhere; checking understanding is the Quiz's job

**Why:** ADR 0019's style guide had the Reading author end most sections with a question the section had just answered ("Can you explain why…?"), and open the Lesson with a question it would answer. In the SEN-48 Gram-stain Course every section ended that way, and as a Learner it read as being tested mid-explanation, with no way to answer and nothing done with the answer. A teacher in a room asks those questions with a tone and a pause that text cannot carry, so in a Reading they come out strange. That goes for questions in the middle of the text, rhetorical ones, and headings too, not only section endings. A Reading explains and the Quiz checks.

We turned down keeping the questions in softer wording, because a rhetorical question still asks the Learner to stop reading and perform. The rule is enforced by a Code check (any `?` in a Reading's prose or headings fails it, and the repair rewrites each question as the statement it was asking for), not left to the author's judgment. The cost is that a Reading loses its built-in pause to recall what it just said, so each section now ends on a plain sentence that sums up its idea. A Reading that has to quote a question, such as the title of a named work, will need a repair to word around it.
