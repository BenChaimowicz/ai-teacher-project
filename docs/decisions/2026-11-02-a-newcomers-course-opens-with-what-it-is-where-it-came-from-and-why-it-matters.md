# A newcomer's Course opens with a Lesson on what the subject is, where it came from, and why it matters

**Why:** In the SEN-48 Gram-stain Course, a Learner who said they were brand new was taken straight into bacterial cell envelopes. They finished the Course without knowing what Gram staining is, who devised it, or why labs use it. Goal focusing had narrowed "Gram staining" to a mechanism goal, and the Blueprint's newcomer rule said only "start from the fundamentals of the Learning Goal", which for a mechanism goal means the mechanism. A person who knows the subject would never teach it that way.

The Blueprint author is now told that a newcomer's first reading Lesson orients them, in plain words: what the subject is, its origin (who and when, where it has that history), and what people use it for. Only then do Lessons turn to how it works. Those points are written as objectives, so the Reading may teach them without the Judge cutting them as off_goal. The Blueprint Judge fails `prerequisite_order` when that Lesson is missing. A sentence or two on why it matters counts as orientation, even when a use such as clinical diagnosis is out of scope.

We turned down making Goal focusing keep an orientation facet in scope, because focusing runs before we know the Learner is new. The cost is one more Lesson in a newcomer's Course, which is more research and writing. The rule applies only to a Learner who says they are new. One who takes the diagnostic and scores at the floor does not get it yet.
