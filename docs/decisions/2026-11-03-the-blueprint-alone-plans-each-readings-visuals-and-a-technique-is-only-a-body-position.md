# The Blueprint alone plans each Reading's visuals, a technique is only a position or movement the body copies, and a file that misses its brief is replaced rather than re-captioned

**Why:** One place decides what a Reading shows. The Blueprint plans each visual's kind and intent, and the Reading writer only writes the brief for it, with no optional images of its own. That way the plan the Blueprint Judge checks is the plan that ships. The plan, the briefs and their `mustShow` checklists stay behind the scenes: the Learner's Blueprint view carries only each Lesson's title and goal, and a Lesson shows only the finished picture and its caption.

A *technique* is narrowed to a position or movement the Learner copies with their body, such as a drumstick grip. We turned down also counting a specimen or result to recognise, such as a Gram stain, as a technique. The prototype's drawn Gram stain taught the purple-versus-pink result clearly, so a result to recognise is a *diagram*: drawn first as a plain schematic, never made to look like a microscope photograph, with real photomicrographs from the library as the fallback. The cost: a Learner may first see a schematic of a stain rather than a real field, and the Judge checks that the drawing never claims to be a photograph.

A library or Commons file is judged against the brief's `mustShow`. One that fails is replaced by the next candidate rather than given a new caption, so caption repair is gone. Other calls confirmed with this: at most two library files are judged per visual, after Commons' three. A drawing, storage or provider failure leaves the Lesson without the picture rather than failing it. A reused Lesson drops its old pictures and briefs its new slot's visuals. The technique Judge is picked by the 13-case drum-grip test set.
