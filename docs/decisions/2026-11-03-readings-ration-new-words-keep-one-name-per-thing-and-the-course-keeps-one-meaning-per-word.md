# A Reading rations new words, keeps one name per thing, gives one win, and the Course keeps one meaning per word

**Why:** A hard read of the third SEN-48 Gram-stain Course (the first written in Chapters) found the voice and the Word list still working against a newcomer, even with the one-screen shape and the kind-friend voice in place. Each 150-word screen introduced five or six new words ("differential stain", "peptidoglycan", "crystal violet–iodine complex", "safranin", "counterstain"), sometimes two in one sentence. The same word meant different things from Lesson to Lesson: "Gram staining" had four definitions across the Course and "Gram-positive" three, and the prose wobbled between "cell wall", "outer structure", "covering", and "envelope", and between "bacterium", "bacterial cell", and "cell". The agent was "people" ("helps people make bacteria visible") and the verbs were nouns ("identification", "investigation", "grouping"). The Course's one phrase, "a first clue", appeared twelve times. The Quiz had tells: the keyed option was the only hedged one in seven items out of ten, five items recycled the same three wrong ideas, and one item turned on 1884 versus 1848 because the orientation rule had made the date an objective.

Yarden asked us to take what works from Matt Pocock's `teach` skill (mattpocock/skills, `skills/productivity/teach`) and fold it into our prompts. We adopted its content rules, which are about working memory and retention, not its workspace: for acquiring knowledge "difficulty is the enemy; it eats working memory you need for understanding", so a lesson is short and gives "a single tangible win"; every lesson is tied to the learner's mission; the glossary is the canonical language, opinionated (one word per thing, the rest are aliases to avoid), with tight definitions of what a term is, used identically in every lesson; and a quiz gives no clue through formatting, order, or the shape of the right answer.

**What changed.** The teaching voice now names who does what and uses one name per thing, the name earlier Lessons used. A new working-memory rule, shared by the author, repair, fit pass, and Judge, lets a Reading introduce at most three words the Learner does not know, never two in one sentence, each glossed where it first appears and used again in the same section. A Reading gives one tangible win, which its closing sentence states, and says each thing once. The Readings now see `learnerAsked`, what the Learner typed in their own words while focusing the goal, and speak to it; the first Lesson answers it plainly. The Word list defines what a thing is, in the word's ordinary meaning, not what it does in that Lesson, and the Course keeps one meaning per word: the word lists of prior Lessons travel with them, the writer is told to copy a known definition, and code overwrites any drift with the first definition whatever the writer wrote. The Quiz keeps the four options alike in confidence as well as length, never reuses one item's wrong ideas in another, and never turns on a date or a name alone; the Judge fails those. The Blueprint plans about three new words per Reading, writes objectives as concrete things to say, tell apart, or do, gives each caveat or use to one Lesson, and phrases the newcomer's orientation ("say what X is and where it came from") so no Quiz tests the year.

**Turned down.** Exactly the same word count in every option (unnatural English; "alike in length, form, and confidence" is the rule). A separate reference document per Course and a visible Course glossary page (the Word list bubbles and the Sources are the reference for now; a Course page is a follow-up). Communities and "wisdom", spaced repetition, and learning records, which are the skill's multi-session workspace and not this product's shape yet.

**Cost.** None in model calls: the rules ride on prompts the Course already pays for, and the prior word lists add a few hundred tokens to each Word list call. The risk is an over-rationed Reading that leaves a needed term unnamed; the Judge's assumed_knowledge and objective_coverage checks pull in the other direction.
