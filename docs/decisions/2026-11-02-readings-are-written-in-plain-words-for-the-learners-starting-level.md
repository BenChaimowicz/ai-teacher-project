# Readings are written in plain words for the Learner's Starting Level, and the Judge fails a Lesson that leans on what they cannot know

**Why:** The Blueprint knew the SEN-48 Learner was brand new, but the Reading author never saw the Starting Level, so every Reading was written the same way at every level. The Gram-stain Lessons came out in textbook register ("peptidoglycan", "mordant", "plasma membrane") and relied on knowledge a newcomer does not have. Plain language was opt-in through the Teaching Profile's `plain_language` support, and a Learner who skipped that question got the dense default.

Now every Reading is plain, everyday English by default, opens on something concrete (a real case, person, or moment from the excerpts), and explains each term where it is first used. The Starting Level, meaning what is in place, the remaining gap, and whether the Learner said they are new, reaches the author, the repair, the reuse fit pass, the word list, and the Judge. The Judge gains an `assumed_knowledge` check.

We turned down keeping plain language as a profile option only, because the level, not a preference, decides whether a Learner can follow the text. The cost is a stricter Judge, which may spend more repairs and fail more Readings, and an expert Learner gets plainer prose than they need.
