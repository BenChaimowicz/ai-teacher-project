# A Reading is about one screen; the Blueprint plans Chapters of short Readings, researched once and drafted together, so the Course costs what it did

**Why:** The second SEN-48 Gram-stain Course had four Readings of 750–900 words and five sections each. Reading one felt like a wall of text, and everything about the subject was crammed into it. The Reading author was sized by the profile's `contentScope` (compact 400–600 words, up to extended 1200–1800), so even the shortest setting was several screens. A Lesson should be about one viewport; a little scrolling is fine, since Lessons carry images, but when an idea cannot be explained in roughly that space, the Course gets another Lesson, not a longer one.

A Reading is now about one screen: 2–4 short sections (shown as paper notes), about 150–250 words of prose, and never more than the hard cap in `packages/db/src/reading-lesson.ts`, which the Code check enforces and the repair cuts toward. It teaches one idea. The Blueprint plans the Readings as Chapters: a Chapter is one topic of one to four consecutive Readings, at most six Chapters in a Course, and the Lesson cap rises from 6 to 30. `contentScope` moves from the Reading author to the Blueprint, where it means how much of the subject the Course covers (compact: the fewest Chapters and Readings that reach the goal; extended: more Readings, with examples and context), not how long each Lesson is; the Learner-facing question is reworded to match. Reuse no longer compares `contentScope`; instead a published Lesson longer than the cap is not retrieved, because an adapt pass trims but never rewrites.

The Chapter is the unit of cost, so that more Lessons do not mean more spend. In the Course above, the four author calls on the main model were $0.29 of $0.80, the four word-list calls $0.10, and the two repairs $0.07; the Judge, grounding, vetting, and vision checks on the cheap model were $0.03 together. Research ran once per Reading ($0.15) and each Reading paid for its own author call. Now research runs once per Chapter, one author call drafts all of a Chapter's Readings from the same Sources, and the Quiz cadence counts Chapters (a Chapter is what a Reading was when the Learner answered that question), so quizzes do not multiply. The Judge, repairs, images, and word list stay per Lesson, which is where the quality gates belong and where the cheap model does the work; the word-list writer no longer receives the Source excerpts, only the Lesson, so that call stays small. A Chapter of one Reading, a reused Lesson, and a Lesson the fallback author rewrites still go through the single-Lesson path. Pipeline Events gain a `chapter` step for the shared draft; each Lesson keeps its own `reading` step.

We turned down paging a long Lesson section by section in Study with no pipeline change: it keeps one Lesson's objectives, Judge pass, and completion as one big unit, and the owner asked for more Lessons, not more pages. We also turned down writing each short Lesson with its own author call, the first cut of this, because it would have roughly doubled the Course (every short Lesson pays the full Sources in its prompt). The cost that remains is per-Lesson repairs and cheap-model checks, so a Course lands near, not exactly at, what it cost before; Lessons are written one after another, so a Course of more Lessons takes somewhat longer; and every Reading published before today is too long for reuse, so reuse starts over.
