# A Reading explains like a kind friend to a curious young person, and never instructs the Learner

**Why:** The second SEN-48 Gram-stain Course read like a lab manual: "Start with a typical Gram-negative cell and trace its three main envelope layers", "Follow a typical Gram-positive cell through the next step", and sentences such as "Keep a first description separate from a precise identification" and "Keep the terms cell wall and cell envelope separate as you compare them", which read as instructions to a writer that leaked into the text. The Pipeline Events show they were in the author's first draft, not a repair: the style guide told the author to end each section on a sentence that sums up its idea and to open each section by saying how it follows from the one before, and the model wrote those as commands to the reader. The language was still too stiff for a newcomer.

The voice is now that of a kind, knowledgeable friend explaining something to one curious young person they like: warm, plain, unhurried, never talking down. A Reading explains and never instructs: no sentence tells the Learner what to do with the text or their own thinking ("Keep X and Y separate", "Note that", "Remember", "Now follow…"); it says the idea itself. Imperatives belong only to a real-world procedure the Lesson teaches. The summing-up and section-link rules are gone; a Lesson ends on one sentence that says its idea in a nutshell, as a statement about the subject. A new Judge check, `instructs_learner`, fails a sentence that reads like a manual, a study tip, or a prompt, and the repair rewrites it as the statement it points at or cuts it.

We turned down a Code check on sentence openers such as "Keep" or "Note", because the same words open legitimate steps of a procedure ("Keep the lens from touching the slide"). The cost is one more thing the Judge can fail, so more repairs, and a voice that an expert Learner may find gentler than they need.
